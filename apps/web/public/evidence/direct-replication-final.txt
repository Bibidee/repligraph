SOURCE STUDY: Controlled caffeine reaction-time experiment
TARGET STUDY: Replication of controlled caffeine reaction-time experiment
Research question: Both studies evaluate whether 100 mg oral caffeine reduces mean simple visual reaction time in healthy adults aged 18 to 35 compared with placebo.
Population: Both studies use healthy adults aged 18 to 35.
Design: Both studies use randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled designs.
Caffeine preparation: 100 mg oral caffeine.
Comparator: Placebo.
Pre-test condition: 12-hour caffeine abstinence.
Measurement timing: 45 minutes after dosing.
Outcome: Mean simple visual reaction time in milliseconds.
Reaction-time protocol: Computerized 50-trial simple visual reaction-time task.
Observed result: Both studies report lower mean reaction time in the caffeine group than in the placebo group.
Replication context: The target study repeats the source research question, population, intervention, comparator, timing, outcome, and reaction-time protocol.
